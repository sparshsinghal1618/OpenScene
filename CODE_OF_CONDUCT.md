# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We want contributing to OpenScene to be a good experience for everyone,
regardless of experience level, age, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or
expression, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual
identity and orientation.

## What is expected

- **Be respectful.** Disagreement is normal and welcome. Rudeness is not.
- **Assume good faith.** Most confusing questions come from someone who is
  missing context, not from someone acting in bad faith.
- **Criticise the work, not the person.** "This approach has a performance
  problem because X" is useful. "This is a terrible idea" is not.
- **Be patient with newcomers.** Everyone was new once. The project has
  specific design rules (see `CONTRIBUTING.md`) that a first-time contributor
  cannot be expected to guess.
- **Accept that maintainers make the final call.** You are welcome to argue
  your case; you are not entitled to have it accepted.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment of any kind, public or private.
- Insults, slurs, or personal attacks.
- Sexualised language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention.
- Deliberately derailing discussions, or sustained disruption of issues and
  reviews after being asked to stop.
- Publishing someone's private information without permission.
- Dismissing a contribution because of who wrote it rather than what it does.

## Scope

This applies to all project spaces: the repository, issues, pull requests,
discussions, and any place where you are representing the project. It also
applies to private communication if it arises from project activity.

## Reporting

Report unacceptable behaviour by opening a private report through the
repository's **Security** tab, or by contacting the maintainer at the email
address on their GitHub profile. Reports are handled confidentially.

Please include what happened, where, and links or screenshots if you have them.
You will get a response as soon as reasonably possible.

## Consequences

Depending on severity, a response may be:

1. A private warning explaining why the behaviour was unacceptable.
2. A temporary ban from project spaces.
3. A permanent ban from project spaces.

Maintainers who do not follow this code in their own conduct face the same
consequences as anyone else.
